Examples

Just give it a try.

The best way to explain a custom program is with an example. So I made five. The businesses are fictional, but the programs really work: feel free to click around.

01Booking an appointment 02Booking a photo shoot 03Ordering online 04Dashboard 05Chat assistant
01

Appointments at the hairdresser

Say you are a hairdresser and your phone rings while you are cutting hair. With this program customers choose a treatment and a free time slot themselves, also in the evening or at the weekend.

It knows how long each treatment takes, when your lunch break is and what time you close. So an appointment that doesn't fit can't be booked.

Works just as well for a garage, a physiotherapist or a beauty salon.

02

Putting together a photo shoot

A photographer almost always gets the same first question: how much is it? Here the customer puts together their own shoot and sees the price right away.

Private customers see the price including VAT, businesses without. The photographer receives a request with everything on it, including the preferred date.

The same idea suits a DJ, a driving school or a caterer.

03

Ordering online at the chip shop

On Friday evening the queue goes out the door. Here customers order in advance and choose a pick-up time, only within opening hours.

Order for a pick-up soon, and you can follow when your order goes into the fryer and when it is ready. Payment simply happens at the counter.

Also useful for a bakery, a butcher or a caterer.

04

Your figures on one screen

Many business owners gather their figures from the till, the accounts and an Excel file. A dashboard puts it all together: revenue, orders and what sells best.

Choose a week, month or year, and hover over the chart for details.

Handy for a shop, a webshop or a restaurant.

05

An assistant that answers day and night

It's ten in the evening and someone wants to know if the salon is open on Saturday, or how much colouring costs. A chat assistant on your website answers right away and even books an appointment.

It only knows what the business teaches it. If it doesn't know the answer, it passes the question on instead of guessing.
